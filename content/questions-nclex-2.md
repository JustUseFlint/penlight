@topic Psychosocial Integrity
@tag PS1
Q: A client says, "I feel like nothing is ever going to get better." Which response by the nurse is most therapeutic?
- "Everything will work out."
- "Why do you feel that way?"
+ "It sounds like you're feeling hopeless. Tell me more."
- "You should try to think positively."
R: Reflecting feelings and using an open-ended invitation encourages the client to share. False reassurance, "why," and advice are blocks to communication.
Q: A client with schizophrenia says, "The voices are telling me to hurt myself." What should the nurse do first?
- Tell the client the voices are not real
- Assign the client to a quiet room alone
+ Stay with the client and assess the content and intent of the command hallucinations
- Document the statement in the chart
R: Command hallucinations to self-harm are a safety risk. Stay with the client, assess intent, and keep the environment safe.
Q: Which statements by the nurse demonstrate therapeutic communication? Select all that apply.
+ "What would you like to talk about today?"
+ "You seem upset. Can you tell me more?"
- "You shouldn't worry about that."
- "Why did you stop taking your medication?"
- "I think you should talk to your family."
R: Open-ended questions and reflection are therapeutic. Dismissing, "why" questions, and advice are not.
Q: A client who is angry and pacing is yelling at staff. What is the best initial nurse response?
- Touch the client's arm to calm them
+ Speak calmly in a low voice, maintain a safe distance, and set clear limits
- Insist the client sit down immediately
- Leave the client alone
R: De-escalation uses a calm tone, personal space, and clear limits, protecting safety for all.
@tag PS2

- Projection
+ Denial
- Regression
- Displacement
R: Denial is refusing to acknowledge a painful reality.
Q: A client's spouse died last week. The client is angry at the nurse for small things. Which is the most likely explanation?
- The client is being manipulative
+ The client is experiencing the anger stage of grief
- The client has a personality disorder
- The client is in denial
R: Anger is a common part of grief. The nurse responds with acceptance.
Q: A nurse suspects that an elderly client is being abused by a caregiver. What is the nurse's responsibility?
- Confront the caregiver
+ Ensure safety, document objectively, and report according to mandatory reporting laws
- Wait for further evidence
- Ignore it since the client has not complained
R: Nurses are mandated reporters. Safety and objective documentation come first.
Q: A client is admitted after a suicide attempt. Which nursing action has the highest priority?
- Encourage the client to talk about feelings
+ Maintain a safe environment with close observation
- Teach coping skills
- Contact the family
R: Safety is the priority. Remove hazards and provide constant or close observation.
@tag PS3
Q: A client taking lithium has a level of 1.8 mEq/L and reports vomiting, diarrhea, and a coarse tremor. The nurse should
- give the next scheduled dose.
+ hold the medication and notify the provider.
- encourage the client to restrict fluids.
- reassure the client.
R: A level above 1.2 mEq/L with these signs indicates lithium toxicity. Hold the drug and notify the provider.
Q: A client taking an MAOI should avoid which food?
- Apples
+ Aged cheese
- Rice
- Fresh chicken
R: Tyramine-rich foods can cause a hypertensive crisis in clients taking MAOIs.
Q: A client admitted for alcohol use disorder had the last drink 12 hours ago. Which findings would the nurse expect? Select all that apply.
+ Tremor
+ Anxiety
+ Tachycardia
+ Diaphoresis
- Bradycardia
- Drowsiness
R: Alcohol withdrawal causes autonomic hyperactivity, not slowing.
Q: A client receiving haloperidol develops a temperature of 104°F (40°C), muscle rigidity, and confusion. Which condition does the nurse suspect?
- Serotonin syndrome
- Tardive dyskinesia
+ Neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- Lithium toxicity
R: NMS is a medical emergency with hyperthermia, rigidity, and altered mental status after antipsychotic use.
@topic Basic Care & Comfort
@tag BC1
Q: A client has an area of intact skin over the sacrum that is red and does not blanch with pressure. What stage is this pressure injury?
+ Stage 1
- Stage 2
- Stage 3
- Unstageable
R: Non-blanchable erythema of intact skin is Stage 1.
Q: What is the best way to prevent pressure injuries in a bedbound client?
- Massage reddened bony areas
+ Reposition at least every 2 hours
- Use a donut cushion
- Limit fluids
R: Regular repositioning relieves pressure. Massage of reddened areas can damage tissue.
Q: A client with right-sided weakness is learning to use a cane. In which hand should the client hold the cane?
- Right hand
+ Left hand
- Either hand
- Both hands
R: The cane goes on the stronger side, opposite the weak leg, to support the weak side.
Q: Which interventions help prevent skin breakdown? Select all that apply.
+ Keeping skin clean and dry
+ Using pressure-relieving mattresses
+ Ensuring adequate protein intake
- Dragging the client up in bed
- Positioning directly on a reddened area
R: Moisture control, pressure relief, nutrition, and avoiding shear and friction prevent breakdown.
@tag BC2
Q: A client receiving continuous enteral feedings is at risk for aspiration. Which position is best?
- Supine
+ Head of the bed elevated at least 30 to 45 degrees
- Left side-lying flat
- Trendelenburg
R: Elevating the head of the bed reduces reflux and aspiration.
Q: A client has a new colostomy. Which stoma finding should be reported to the provider immediately?
- Pink and moist
- Slight swelling
+ Dark, purple-black color
- Small amount of bleeding when cleaned
R: A dusky or black stoma suggests ischemia and requires immediate attention.
Q: A client with chronic kidney disease should be taught to limit which foods?
- Apples and rice
+ Bananas, oranges, and potatoes (high in potassium)
- Pasta
- Green beans
R: Renal clients often need potassium and phosphorus restriction to avoid hyperkalemia.
Q: To reduce the risk of catheter-associated urinary tract infection, the nurse should
- irrigate the catheter daily.
+ keep the drainage bag below bladder level and remove the catheter as soon as it is not needed.
- keep the bag on the bed.
- disconnect the tubing to empty the bag.
R: Gravity drainage and limiting catheter duration reduce infection.
@tag BC3

- Delay the medication
- Offer only non-pharmacologic measures
+ Assess the pain and administer the ordered analgesic
- Ask the family to decide
R: Pain is what the client says it is. Undertreating pain for fear of addiction is inappropriate.
Q: A client receiving morphine has a respiratory rate of 8. What is the nurse's first action?
- Document the rate
+ Stimulate the client and prepare to give naloxone as ordered
- Increase the morphine
- Reassess in 1 hour
R: A respiratory rate below 12 with sedation suggests opioid-induced respiratory depression.
Q: A nurse is caring for a dying client. Which observations are expected near death? Select all that apply.
+ Mottled skin on the extremities
+ Cheyne-Stokes respirations
+ Decreased oral intake
- Increased appetite
- Bright rosy cheeks and increased activity
R: Declining circulation, irregular breathing, and decreased intake are common near death.
Q: Which intervention provides the best comfort for the client with end-stage illness?
- Aggressive physical therapy
+ Pain control, mouth care, and emotional support for client and family
- Strict bedrest
- Restricting visitors
R: Comfort-focused care is the priority of end-of-life care.
@topic Pharmacological & Parenteral Therapies
@tag PH1
Q: Which two identifiers should the nurse use before administering a medication?
- Room number and name
+ Name and date of birth
- Diagnosis and room number
- Date of admission and name
R: Use two patient-specific identifiers; the room number is not an identifier.
Q: A nurse is about to give a client a new antibiotic. What should the nurse verify first?
- The cost of the medication
+ The client's allergies
- The client's diet
- The client's visitor list
R: Allergy verification prevents severe reactions.
Q: Which medication should NEVER be given by IV push?
- Furosemide
- Ondansetron
+ Concentrated potassium chloride
- Morphine
R: Undiluted potassium can cause fatal cardiac arrest.
Q: Which instructions are correct for administering medications? Select all that apply.
+ Do not crush extended-release tablets
+ Allow sublingual tablets to dissolve under the tongue
- Mix all crushed pills in applesauce regardless of type
- Leave medications at the bedside for the client to take later
R: Altering extended-release drugs can cause dangerous overdosing, and medications are administered, not left.
@tag PH2
Q: During a blood transfusion, the client develops fever, chills, and back pain. What is the nurse's first action?
- Slow the infusion
- Administer acetaminophen
+ Stop the transfusion and keep the IV open with normal saline using new tubing
- Flush the blood tubing
R: Stop the transfusion, maintain IV access with saline, and notify the provider.
Q: Which IV fluid should be used with blood products?
- 5% dextrose in water
- Lactated Ringer's
+ 0.9% sodium chloride
- 0.45% sodium chloride
R: Only normal saline is compatible; other fluids can cause hemolysis or clotting.
Q: The nurse notes swelling, coolness, and pallor at the IV insertion site. What should the nurse do?
- Slow the infusion
+ Stop the infusion and remove the IV catheter
- Apply a warm compress and continue infusion
- Flush the IV
R: These are signs of infiltration; stop the infusion and remove the catheter.
Q: Which assessment findings suggest fluid volume overload from IV therapy? Select all that apply.
+ Crackles in the lungs
+ Jugular vein distention
+ Dyspnea
- Dry mucous membranes
- Decreased blood pressure
R: Overload causes congestion and high-volume signs rather than dehydration.

Q: The nurse prepares to give metoprolol. The client's heart rate is 52 beats per minute. What should the nurse do?

- Give half the dose
- Give the dose with food

- naloxone.
- flumazenil.
+ acetylcysteine.

R: Acetylcysteine reduces liver damage from acetaminophen overdose.
Q: A client taking warfarin has an INR of 5.2 and reports blood in the urine. Which medication should the nurse anticipate?
- Protamine sulfate
+ Vitamin K
- Naloxone
- Heparin
R: Vitamin K reverses warfarin. Protamine reverses heparin.
Q: A client taking digoxin has a potassium of 3.0 mEq/L and nausea with yellow-green vision. What should the nurse suspect?
- Digoxin deficiency
+ Digoxin toxicity
- Hyperkalemia
- Allergic reaction to digoxin

@topic Reduction of Risk Potential
@tag RR1
Q: Which laboratory result is the best indicator of kidney function?
- Hemoglobin
+ Serum creatinine
- WBC count
- Sodium
R: Creatinine is relatively stable and is the best marker of kidney function.
Q: A client's INR is 2.5 while on warfarin for atrial fibrillation. What does the nurse conclude?
- The dose is too low
- The dose is too high
+ The INR is in the therapeutic range
- The client needs vitamin K

Q: A client has a hemoglobin of 6.5 g/dL. Which finding does the nurse expect?
+ Fatigue, pallor, and tachycardia
- Hypertension
- Bradycardia
- Flushed skin
R: Severe anemia reduces oxygen delivery, causing fatigue, pallor, and compensatory tachycardia.
Q: Which values would be reported to the provider immediately? Select all that apply.
+ Potassium 6.4 mEq/L
+ Platelets 18,000/mm³
- Sodium 138 mEq/L
+ Glucose 38 mg/dL
- Hemoglobin 13.5 g/dL
R: Critical values require immediate notification; the others are normal.

Q: Which interventions help prevent postoperative pneumonia? Select all that apply.

- Keeping the client supine
- Restricting all fluids
R: Lung expansion, mobility, and mobilizing secretions prevent atelectasis and pneumonia.

Q: Which finding is the earliest sign of shock?

@topic Physiological Adaptation
@tag PA1
Q: ABG results: pH 7.28, PaCO2 56, HCO3 24. Which is the correct interpretation?
- Metabolic acidosis
+ Respiratory acidosis
- Respiratory alkalosis
- Metabolic alkalosis
R: Low pH with high CO2 (respiratory acid) and normal HCO3 is uncompensated respiratory acidosis.
Q: ABG: pH 7.52, PaCO2 30, HCO3 24. Which condition is most likely?
- Respiratory acidosis
+ Respiratory alkalosis
- Metabolic acidosis
- Normal
R: High pH with low CO2 is respiratory alkalosis, such as in hyperventilation from anxiety.
Q: A client with DKA is breathing deeply and rapidly. The nurse understands this is the body's attempt to
+ blow off CO2 to compensate for metabolic acidosis.
- increase oxygen.
- cause alkalosis.
- relieve anxiety.
R: Kussmaul respirations compensate for acidosis by eliminating CO2.
Q: Which conditions can cause metabolic alkalosis? Select all that apply.
+ Prolonged vomiting
+ Nasogastric suction
- Diarrhea
- Diabetic ketoacidosis
R: Loss of gastric acid raises pH; diarrhea and DKA cause acidosis.

+ Crackles
+ Orthopnea
+ Cough that is worse at night
- Peripheral edema as the primary sign
- Jugular vein distention as the primary sign

- Give insulin

- offer water.

- shock.
+ Cushing's triad, a sign of increased ICP.
- anxiety.
- sepsis.
R: Hypertension with widened pulse pressure, bradycardia, and irregular respirations indicate late increased ICP.
Q: Which actions should the nurse take for a client who is having a tonic-clonic seizure? Select all that apply.
+ Protect the head
+ Turn the client to the side after the convulsions stop
+ Note the time and duration
- Place a tongue blade in the mouth
- Restrain the client's arms
R: Protect from injury and maintain airway. Never put objects in the mouth or restrain the client.
Q: Which findings differentiate DKA from HHS? Select all that apply.
+ DKA has ketones and acidosis
+ HHS has very high glucose without significant ketosis
- HHS occurs mainly in type 1 diabetes
- DKA is not associated with dehydration
R: DKA is typically type 1 with ketoacidosis; HHS is typically type 2 with extreme hyperglycemia.
