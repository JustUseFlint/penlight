Q: "Sleep is not a luxury. Adults who sleep fewer than six hours a night are more likely to develop high blood pressure, gain weight, and have trouble concentrating. Studies also show that sleep helps the brain store new memories." Which sentence best states the main idea of the passage?
- Adults who sleep less than six hours often gain weight.
+ Adequate sleep is important for physical health and brain function.
- The brain stores memories during the day.
- Sleep is a luxury that busy people can skip.
R: The passage gives several benefits of sleep (blood pressure, weight, concentration, memory) and opens by saying sleep is not a luxury. The main idea must cover all of them. One option is too narrow (weight only), one contradicts the text, and one is not stated.
Q: A paragraph describes three ways hospitals reduce falls: bed alarms, non-slip socks, and hourly rounding. Which would be the best topic sentence?
- Falls are the leading cause of death in hospitals.
+ Hospitals use several simple strategies to prevent patient falls.
- Non-slip socks are the most effective fall prevention tool.
- Hourly rounding takes up too much nursing time.
R: A topic sentence states the main idea that all the supporting details share. Option B covers all three strategies. The others are too narrow, not supported, or add opinions.
Q: Supporting details in a passage are best described as
- the author's main point.
- the title of the passage.
+ facts, examples, or reasons that back up the main idea.
- the final sentence of each paragraph.
R: Supporting details give evidence for the main idea. The main idea itself is the central point, and a supporting detail can appear anywhere in the passage.
Q: A passage about handwashing states: "Handwashing prevents infection. Washing for 20 seconds with soap removes most germs. In hospitals, regular handwashing has lowered infection rates." Which sentence is the main idea?
+ Handwashing prevents infection.
- Washing for 20 seconds with soap removes most germs.
- Hospitals have lowered infection rates.
- Soap is better than water alone.
R: The first sentence is a general claim, and the other sentences give supporting evidence for it.
Q: Which answer choice to a main-idea question is MOST likely wrong?
- One that is supported by the passage and covers all paragraphs.
+ One that is true in real life but is not stated or supported in the passage.
- One that paraphrases the author's central claim.
- One that matches the title and the topic sentences.
R: The TEAS wants answers supported by the passage. An answer that is true in the real world but not in the text is a classic distractor.

Q: Which signal words indicate a compare-and-contrast text structure?
- first, next, finally
- because, therefore, as a result
+ however, similarly, on the other hand
- for example, such as
R: However, similarly, and on the other hand signal comparisons or contrasts. First/next/finally signal sequence, and because/therefore signal cause and effect.

- description.
+ cause and effect.
- problem and solution only.

Q: A bar graph shows flu vaccination rates by age group, with the 65+ group at 70% and the 18-34 group at 35%. Which statement is supported?
- The 18-34 group had the highest rate.
+ The 65+ group's rate was twice that of the 18-34 group.

- Younger adults refuse to be vaccinated.

Q: Which of the following is the BEST example of evidence supporting a claim that a new exercise program improves health?
- A neighbor says she feels great.
+ A controlled study showing lower blood pressure in participants after 12 weeks.
- A celebrity endorsement.
- An advertisement with smiling people.
R: Controlled studies with measurable outcomes are strong evidence. Testimonials and endorsements are anecdotal.

+ Article A, because it relies on independent research.
- Article B, because manufacturers know the drug best.
- Both are equally reliable.
- Neither, because all articles are biased.
R: Independent research evidence is more reliable than a source that may have a financial interest.

- The font size
R: Misreading units or axes is a common error, so check the labels first.
