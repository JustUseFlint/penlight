@deck teas-reading | TEAS Reading Essentials | Reading
@topic Reading
@tag R1
Main idea => The central point the author makes about the topic.
Supporting detail => A fact, example, or reason that backs up the main idea.
Topic sentence => The sentence that states a paragraph's main idea (often first or last).
Main-idea answer that is "too narrow" => True, but covers only one detail of the passage.
@tag R2
Inference => A conclusion drawn from evidence in the text when the author does not state it directly.
Author's purpose: persuade => Uses opinion words, emotional appeals, and a call to act.
Author's purpose: inform => Neutral language and verifiable facts.
Tone => The author's attitude toward the subject, shown through word choice.
@tag R3
Signal words for sequence => First, next, then, finally, afterward.
Signal words for compare/contrast => However, similarly, on the other hand, unlike, both.
Signal words for cause and effect => Because, therefore, as a result, leads to.
Fact vs. opinion => A fact can be verified; an opinion expresses a belief or judgment.
Bias => A one-sided presentation that ignores other viewpoints.
@tag R4
Claim => What an author wants the reader to believe.
Strong evidence => Relevant, sufficient, accurate facts or data (such as controlled studies).
First thing to check on a graph => Title, axis labels, and units.
Overgeneralization => Drawing a broad conclusion from a small or unrepresentative sample.
@tag R5
Definition context clue => The word's meaning is stated right in the sentence, often after "or" or "that is".
Prefix hypo- => Under or below.
Prefix hyper- => Over or above.
Root brady- => Slow.
Root tachy- => Fast.
Suffix -itis => Inflammation.
Suffix -ectomy => Surgical removal.
Suffix -emia => Condition of the blood.
@deck teas-math | TEAS Math Essentials | Math
@topic Math
@tag M1
Order of operations => Parentheses, exponents, multiplication/division (left to right), addition/subtraction (left to right).
Negative × negative => Positive.
Prime number => A number with exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
@tag M2
How to divide fractions => Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
Adding fractions with unlike denominators => Find a common denominator, then add the numerators.
Fraction to decimal => Divide the numerator by the denominator.
@tag M3
Part = ? => Percent × Whole.
Percent change formula => (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100.
Sale price with 20% off => Original × 0.80.
Whole when part and percent are known => Part ÷ Percent (as a decimal).
@tag M4
Proportion => An equation stating that two ratios are equal; solve by cross-multiplying.
Unit rate => A rate with 1 in the denominator (such as $3 per item).
@tag M5
Rule for inequalities => Reverse the sign when multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative.
y = mx + b => m is the slope (rate of change); b is the y-intercept.
"Less than" in word problems => Subtraction, written in reverse order ("5 less than x" is x − 5).
@tag M6
1 kg => 1,000 g.
1 g => 1,000 mg.
1 mg => 1,000 mcg.
1 L => 1,000 mL.
1 inch => 2.54 cm.
1 kg in pounds => About 2.2 lb.
1 tablespoon => 3 teaspoons (about 15 mL).
1 teaspoon => About 5 mL.
1 cup => 8 fluid ounces.
@tag M7
Mean => Sum of values ÷ number of values.
Median => Middle value after ordering (average the two middle values if the count is even).
Mode => The most frequently occurring value.
Range => Highest value − lowest value.
Area of a circle => πr².
Area of a triangle => ½ × base × height.
Probability => Favorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.
@deck teas-science | TEAS Science: A&P, Life and Physical Science | Science
@topic Science
@tag S1
Which side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs? => The right side (right ventricle via the pulmonary artery).
Blood flow through the heart (valves) => RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonic → lungs → LA → mitral → LV → aortic → aorta.
Arteries vs. veins => Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it toward the heart.
Site of gas exchange => The alveoli.
Platelets => Cell fragments that help blood clot.
Red blood cells carry => Oxygen (bound to hemoglobin).
Diaphragm during inhalation => Contracts and moves down, increasing chest volume.
@tag S2
CNS includes => The brain and spinal cord.
Cerebellum function => Balance and coordination.
Hypothalamus function => Temperature, hunger, thirst, and hormone control.
Sympathetic nervous system => "Fight or flight": increases heart rate, dilates pupils.
Parasympathetic nervous system => "Rest and digest": decreases heart rate, increases digestion.
Insulin => Hormone from the pancreas that lowers blood glucose.
Glucagon => Hormone from the pancreas that raises blood glucose.
Pituitary gland => The "master gland" that controls many other endocrine glands.
Thyroxine => Thyroid hormone that regulates metabolism.
@tag S3
Main site of nutrient absorption => The small intestine.
Main function of the large intestine => Absorbing water and forming feces.
Amylase => Enzyme that digests starch (carbohydrate).
Pepsin => Stomach enzyme that digests protein.
Bile is made in the ___ and stored in the ___ => Made in the liver; stored in the gallbladder.
Functional unit of the kidney => The nephron.
Path of urine => Kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra.
Where fertilization occurs => The fallopian tube.
@tag S4
Tendon vs. ligament => Tendon: muscle to bone. Ligament: bone to bone.
Three types of muscle => Skeletal (voluntary), smooth (involuntary), cardiac (heart).
Layers of the skin => Epidermis, dermis, hypodermis.
Active immunity => Your body makes antibodies (infection or vaccine).
Passive immunity => Antibodies are received from another source (mother's milk, injection).
B cells => Produce antibodies.
T cells => Attack infected cells and help coordinate immune responses.
@tag S5
Mitochondria => Produce ATP, the cell's usable energy.
Ribosomes => Build proteins.
Lysosomes => Digest waste and cellular debris.
Golgi apparatus => Modifies, packages, and ships proteins.
Cell wall (plants) => Rigid outer layer for support; absent in animal cells.
Diffusion vs. active transport => Diffusion: high to low, no energy. Active transport: low to high, uses ATP.
Osmosis => Diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane.
@tag S6
Genotype vs. phenotype => Genotype: the alleles; phenotype: the observable trait.
Heterozygous => Two different alleles for a trait (Aa).
Bb × Bb offspring ratio => 25% BB, 50% Bb, 25% bb.
DNA base pairs => A–T and C–G (RNA uses U instead of T).
Mitosis vs. meiosis => Mitosis: two identical body cells. Meiosis: four sex cells with half the chromosomes.
Antibiotics work on => Bacteria, not viruses.
@tag S7
Atomic number => The number of protons.
Isotopes => Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
Ionic bond => Electrons are transferred between atoms.
Covalent bond => Electrons are shared between atoms.
Example of a chemical change => Rusting, burning, digestion (a new substance forms).
@tag S8
Acidic pH range => Below 7.
Basic pH range => Above 7.
Normal blood pH => 7.35 to 7.45.
Each pH unit change => A tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration.
Solute vs. solvent => Solute: the dissolved substance. Solvent: the substance that dissolves it (water).
@tag S9
Independent variable => The factor the experimenter changes.
Dependent variable => The factor that is measured.
Control group => A group that does not receive the treatment, used for comparison.
Correlation vs. causation => Two things occurring together does not prove one caused the other.
@deck teas-english | TEAS English & Language Essentials | English & Language
@topic English & Language
@tag E1
Subject-verb agreement rule => Singular subjects take singular verbs; ignore words between subject and verb.
Singular indefinite pronouns => Each, everyone, somebody, either, neither.
Noun => A person, place, thing, or idea.
Adverb => Describes a verb, adjective, or other adverb (often ends in -ly).
@tag E2
Comma splice => Two independent clauses joined by only a comma.
Semicolon use => Joins two related independent clauses.
Colon use => Introduces a list or explanation after a complete sentence.
FANBOYS => For, and, nor, but, or, yet, so: coordinating conjunctions.
Its vs. it's => Its is possessive; it's means it is.
@tag E3
Affect vs. effect => Affect is usually a verb (to influence); effect is usually a noun (a result).
Their, there, they're => Possessive; place; they are.
Then vs. than => Then: time. Than: comparison.
Homophones => Words that sound alike but differ in spelling and meaning.
@tag E4
Parallel structure => Using the same grammatical form for items in a list.
Active voice => The subject performs the action ("The nurse gave the medication").
Topic sentence => States the main idea of a paragraph.
Contrast transitions => However, but, on the other hand, nevertheless.
@tag E5
Who vs. whom => Who is the subject; whom is the object.
Dangling modifier => A modifier with no clear word to modify.
Pronoun-antecedent agreement => A pronoun must match its antecedent in number and person.
Past participle => The verb form used with have/has/had (written, spoken, gone).
