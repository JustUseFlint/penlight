Q: A nurse reads this NCLEX question: "Which statement by the client indicates a need for further teaching?" What is the nurse being asked to identify?
- The most correct statement
+ An incorrect statement that shows a misunderstanding
- The statement the nurse would say
- The most important statement
R: "Further teaching needed" questions ask for the INCORRECT response, because a wrong statement reveals what the client still needs to learn.
Q: Which strategy is MOST helpful when answering a select-all-that-apply question?
- Choose exactly three options
+ Treat each option as an individual true-or-false statement
- Select the longest options
- Skip it and guess at the end
R: Each SATA option is evaluated independently. There is no set number of correct answers.
Q: The nurse sees the words "priority" and "initial" in a question stem. These key words indicate the nurse should
- choose the answer that is the most comfortable for the client.
+ identify the action that should be done first or is most important.
- choose any correct intervention.
- pick the most detailed answer.
R: Priority and first are cues to rank the options, usually by ABCs, safety, acuity, and the nursing process.
@tag ST2
Q: A client with a history of COPD reports chest tightness and has a respiratory rate of 32. Which action should the nurse take first?
- Obtain a 12-lead ECG
- Notify the provider
+ Assess airway and oxygen saturation, and position the client upright
- Document the finding
R: ABCs: airway and breathing come first. Assess and support breathing before further testing or notifications.
Q: Which client need is highest priority according to Maslow's hierarchy?
- Belonging
+ Oxygenation
- Self-esteem
- Privacy
R: Physiological needs such as oxygen, fluids, and nutrition come before safety, belonging, and esteem.
Q: A nurse is caring for four clients. Which client should the nurse assess FIRST?
- A client with a stable fracture waiting for a cast
+ A postoperative client with new restlessness and an oxygen saturation of 88%
- A client with diabetes who has a blood glucose of 150 mg/dL
- A client requesting a pain medication for chronic back pain
R: New restlessness and low oxygen saturation suggest hypoxia, an airway/breathing problem. It is acute and unstable.
Q: Which step of the nursing process involves analyzing collected data to identify client problems?
- Assessment
+ Diagnosis
- Planning
- Evaluation
R: In the diagnosis step, the nurse analyzes assessment data and forms nursing diagnoses.
@tag MC1
Q: Which task is most appropriate for the RN to delegate to an unlicensed assistive personnel (UAP)?
- Teaching a client how to use an inhaler
+ Measuring and recording intake and output for a stable client
- Assessing a client's lung sounds after surgery
- Evaluating a client's response to pain medication
R: UAP can perform routine, predictable tasks such as I&O. Teaching, assessment, and evaluation require the RN.
Q: Which clients may the charge nurse assign to an LPN/LVN? Select all that apply.
+ A stable client with pneumonia receiving oral antibiotics
+ A client needing a dressing change on a healing wound
- A newly admitted client needing an initial admission assessment
- A client with unstable vital signs after surgery
- A client who needs teaching about a new diagnosis of diabetes
R: LPNs care for stable clients with predictable outcomes. Initial assessment, teaching new diagnoses, and unstable clients stay with the RN.
Q: The RN delegates ambulation of a client after hip surgery to a UAP. Which instruction is most important?
- "Walk the client as far as possible."
+ "Use a gait belt and report any dizziness or pain."
- "Make sure the client eats afterward."
- "Chart the client's mobility as complete."
R: Safety and communication of what to report are key elements of delegation.
Q: Which task can the RN NOT delegate to an LPN?
- Administering an oral medication to a stable client
- Reinforcing previously taught information
+ Developing the initial plan of care
- Performing a sterile dressing change
R: Care planning, initial assessment, and teaching new content are RN responsibilities.

- Proceed with preoperative preparation
- Ask the client to sign again
+ Notify the surgeon that the client has questions and needs further explanation
- Explain the risks of the surgery to the client

Q: A nurse overhears a coworker discussing a client's diagnosis in the elevator. Which principle is being violated?
- Autonomy
+ Confidentiality

Q: Which situations require the nurse to ensure that informed consent is obtained? Select all that apply.
+ Insertion of a central venous catheter
+ A surgical procedure
- Taking a client's blood pressure
- Providing a bed bath
+ A blood transfusion
R: Invasive procedures, surgeries, and transfusions require informed consent; routine nursing care does not.
Q: After giving a wrong dose of a medication, the nurse has assessed the client and notified the provider. What should the nurse do next?
- Document the incident report in the client's chart
+ Complete an incident report per facility policy
- Tell the client's family first

Q: During a disaster, which client should be tagged RED (emergent)?
- A client walking with a minor laceration
- A client with a closed forearm fracture
+ A client with a tension pneumothorax and severe respiratory distress
- A client with massive injuries who is not breathing after airway repositioning
R: Red is for life-threatening but salvageable injuries. A client who is not breathing after repositioning is classified as black (expectant).
Q: The nurse is assigned four clients. Which one should be seen FIRST?
- A client with a blood pressure of 150/90 who is scheduled for discharge
+ A client with sudden onset of slurred speech and facial droop
- A client with a stage 2 pressure injury
- A client who needs help with morning care
R: Sudden neurological changes suggest a stroke, a time-sensitive emergency.
Q: Which finding in a client 2 hours after a cardiac catheterization requires the nurse's IMMEDIATE attention?
- Mild groin tenderness
+ Expanding hematoma at the insertion site with a weak distal pulse
- A request for a snack
- A blood pressure of 128/78
R: An expanding hematoma and diminished pulses indicate bleeding and impaired circulation.
Q: Which clients would the nurse expect to need continuous cardiac monitoring? Select all that apply.
+ A client with chest pain and ST changes
+ A client with a new onset of atrial fibrillation with a rapid rate
- A client with a stable ankle sprain
- A client with mild nausea after eating
R: Cardiac rhythm monitoring is for clients with cardiac symptoms or abnormal rhythms.

@tag SI1
Q: Which type of precautions should the nurse implement for a client with suspected pulmonary tuberculosis?
- Contact precautions
- Droplet precautions
+ Airborne precautions in a negative-pressure room
- Standard precautions only
R: TB is spread by airborne particles. The client needs a negative-pressure room, and staff wear an N95 respirator.
Q: A client with C. difficile diarrhea is admitted. Which hand hygiene practice is correct?
- Alcohol-based hand rub is sufficient.
+ Wash hands with soap and water.
- Hand hygiene is only needed after leaving the room.
- Double-glove instead of washing.
R: C. difficile forms spores that alcohol does not kill. Soap and water mechanically remove them.
Q: In what order does the nurse remove PPE when leaving the room of a client on contact precautions?
- Mask, gown, gloves
+ Gloves, goggles, gown, mask
- Gown, gloves, mask
- Gloves, mask, gown, goggles
R: Remove the most contaminated items first (gloves), then eye protection, gown, and mask last. Perform hand hygiene.
Q: Which clients can safely share a room? Select all that apply.
+ Two clients with MRSA infection in wounds
- A client with MRSA and a client with a new surgical wound
+ Two clients with influenza
- A client with TB and a client with influenza
- A neutropenic client and a client with pneumonia
R: Clients with the same organism can be cohorted. A client at risk of infection should not share with an infectious client.
Q: Which illness requires droplet precautions?
- Measles
- Varicella
+ Influenza
- Scabies
R: Influenza spreads by large respiratory droplets. Measles and varicella are airborne, and scabies requires contact precautions.

Q: Which intervention best prevents falls in a confused older client?
- Use bed rails on all four sides
+ Place the client in a room near the nurses' station with a bed alarm and the bed in low position
- Apply wrist restraints
- Keep the lights off to promote sleep
R: Close observation and alarms are least restrictive. Four rails can be a restraint, and restraints raise the risk of injury.
Q: A nurse discovers a small fire in a client's room. What is the FIRST action?
+ Rescue the client in immediate danger
- Pull the fire alarm
- Close the door
- Use the extinguisher
R: RACE begins with Rescue, then Alarm, Contain, Extinguish.
Q: Which finding requires the nurse to take immediate action for a client in wrist restraints?
- The client is resting quietly
+ Cool fingers with a delayed capillary refill
- The restraint knot is a quick-release knot
- The restraint is secured to the bed frame
R: Signs of impaired circulation require immediate loosening and reassessment.
Q: Which are appropriate fall-prevention measures? Select all that apply.
+ Keeping the call light within reach
+ Offering toileting regularly
- Keeping the bed at the highest position for caregiver comfort
+ Ensuring non-skid footwear
R: A low bed, accessible call light, regular toileting, and good footwear reduce falls.

+ Stop the infusion and notify the provider, and prepare calcium gluconate
- Increase the infusion rate
- Document the findings

- Document the finding
+ Assist the client to void, then massage the fundus
- Administer a laxative
- Apply a heating pad
R: A full bladder displaces the uterus and prevents contraction. Empty the bladder and massage the fundus.
Q: A client's blood type is Rh-negative and the baby is Rh-positive. When should the nurse expect Rh immune globulin to be given? Select all that apply.
+ At about 28 weeks of gestation
+ Within 72 hours after delivery

R: RhoGAM prevents sensitization. It is given at about 28 weeks and within 72 hours after birth of an Rh-positive baby.

- Receiving the influenza vaccine
+ A screening mammogram
- Cardiac rehabilitation after a heart attack
- Wearing a seat belt
R: Secondary prevention detects disease early. Vaccines are primary, and rehabilitation is tertiary.

- Ask whether the client understands
+ Ask the client to demonstrate the technique (teach-back)
- Give a written handout only

Q: Which client should NOT receive a live attenuated vaccine?
- A healthy 12-month-old
+ A severely immunocompromised client receiving chemotherapy
- A healthy adult
- A child with a mild cold
R: Live vaccines can cause infection in immunocompromised clients.
Q: Which are examples of primary prevention? Select all that apply.
+ Childhood immunizations
+ Teaching about healthy diet and physical activity
- Colonoscopy screening
- Physical therapy after stroke
R: Primary prevention prevents disease before it occurs.
