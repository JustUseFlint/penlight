@lesson AP1 | Homeostasis, Cells, and Tissues | Anatomy & Physiology | 6
**Homeostasis** is the body's ability to keep a stable internal environment through **negative feedback** loops (the response opposes the change, like sweating when you are hot). **Positive feedback** amplifies a change (labor contractions, clotting).
## Organization
Cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.
## Four tissue types
- **Epithelial:** covers surfaces and lines cavities; secretes and absorbs.
- **Connective:** supports and connects (bone, blood, fat, cartilage).
- **Muscle:** skeletal, smooth, cardiac.
- **Nervous:** neurons and glia that conduct signals.
## Anatomical terms
Anterior (front), posterior (back), superior (above), inferior (below), medial (toward midline), lateral (away from midline), proximal (closer to the trunk), distal (farther).
> Must know: Body temperature regulation is a classic negative feedback loop.

@lesson AP2 | Skin, Bones, and Muscles | Anatomy & Physiology | 6
- The **skin** protects, regulates temperature, senses touch, and makes vitamin D. Layers: epidermis, dermis, hypodermis.
- **Bone** supports, protects, stores calcium, and (red marrow) makes blood cells. Osteoblasts build bone; osteoclasts break it down.
- **Joints:** synovial joints (knee, shoulder) allow free movement; cartilage cushions.
- **Muscle contraction:** nerve signal → calcium release → actin and myosin slide → shortening, using ATP. Skeletal muscle is voluntary; cardiac and smooth muscle are involuntary.
- **Tendons** connect muscle to bone; **ligaments** connect bone to bone.
> Must know: Calcium and vitamin D are essential for bone health; weight-bearing exercise strengthens bone.

@lesson AP3 | The Nervous System | Anatomy & Physiology | 7
- **CNS:** brain and spinal cord. **PNS:** cranial and spinal nerves. The PNS includes the somatic (voluntary) and autonomic (involuntary) divisions.
- **Brain lobes:** frontal (movement, judgment, speech production), parietal (sensation), temporal (hearing, memory), occipital (vision).
- **Brainstem** (midbrain, pons, medulla) controls breathing and heart rate. **Cerebellum** coordinates movement and balance.
- **Autonomic:** sympathetic (fight or flight: ↑ heart rate, dilated pupils, bronchodilation) vs. parasympathetic (rest and digest: ↓ heart rate, ↑ digestion).
- Neuron: dendrites receive, axon transmits; myelin speeds conduction. Neurotransmitters cross synapses (acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine).
- **Cranial nerves** (12) control senses, facial movement, swallowing, and more.
> Must know: A stroke in the left hemisphere typically causes right-sided weakness (nerve fibers cross).

@lesson AP4 | The Endocrine System | Anatomy & Physiology | 6
Hormones travel in blood to target cells and are controlled by feedback.
- **Hypothalamus → pituitary:** the pituitary releases growth hormone, TSH, ACTH, FSH/LH, prolactin; posterior pituitary releases **ADH** and oxytocin.
- **Thyroid:** T3/T4 set metabolic rate. **Parathyroid:** PTH raises blood calcium.
- **Adrenal:** cortex makes cortisol (stress, anti-inflammatory) and aldosterone (sodium and water retention); medulla makes epinephrine.
- **Pancreas:** beta cells make insulin (lowers glucose); alpha cells make glucagon (raises glucose).
- **Examples of imbalance:** too little insulin → diabetes mellitus; too little ADH → diabetes insipidus (large volumes of dilute urine); too little thyroid hormone → hypothyroidism (slow, cold, tired).
> Must know: Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon, cortisol, and epinephrine raise it.

@lesson AP5 | The Cardiovascular System | Anatomy & Physiology | 7
- **Cardiac output** = heart rate × stroke volume. **Blood pressure** = cardiac output × systemic vascular resistance.
- **Preload** is the stretch of the ventricle before it contracts; **afterload** is the resistance it pumps against.
- Electrical conduction: SA node → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibers.
- **ECG:** P wave (atrial depolarization), QRS (ventricular depolarization), T wave (ventricular repolarization).
- Heart sounds: S1 ("lub," AV valves close), S2 ("dub," semilunar valves close).
- Coronary arteries fill during **diastole**.
- Baroreceptors sense blood pressure and trigger reflexes to correct it.
> Must know: Left ventricular failure backs blood into the lungs; right ventricular failure backs blood into the body.

@lesson AP6 | The Respiratory System | Anatomy & Physiology | 6
- Conducting airways: nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles. Gas exchange happens in **alveoli**, kept open by **surfactant**.
- Breathing is controlled by the **medulla and pons**; the main trigger to breathe is rising **CO₂** (falling pH). Chronic CO₂ retainers may rely on low oxygen.
- Lung volumes: tidal volume (a normal breath, about 500 mL), vital capacity (maximum exhale after maximum inhale).
- Oxygen travels bound to **hemoglobin**. The oxyhemoglobin curve shifts right (releasing more O₂) with acidosis, fever, and high CO₂.
- The **diaphragm** is the main muscle of inspiration; exhalation at rest is passive.
> Must know: Surfactant lowers surface tension so alveoli don't collapse; premature infants may lack it.

@lesson AP7 | Digestive and Urinary Systems | Anatomy & Physiology | 7
- **Digestive tract:** mouth → esophagus → stomach → duodenum → jejunum → ileum → colon → rectum. Accessory organs: liver, gallbladder, pancreas.
- Enzymes: amylase (starch), pepsin (protein), lipase (fat). Bile emulsifies fat. The stomach is acidic (pH 1 to 3).
- **Kidney functions:** filter blood, regulate fluid, electrolytes, and acid-base balance, and make **erythropoietin** (red blood cells) and **renin** (blood pressure).
- **Nephron:** glomerulus (filtration) → proximal tubule (reabsorption) → loop of Henle (concentration) → distal tubule and collecting duct (fine adjustment by ADH and aldosterone).
- **Normal urine output** is at least about 30 mL/hr (0.5 mL/kg/hr).
> Must know: Aldosterone makes the kidney retain sodium and water and excrete potassium.

@lesson AP8 | Immune, Lymphatic, and Reproductive Systems | Anatomy & Physiology | 6
- **Innate immunity:** skin, mucous membranes, inflammation, phagocytes, fever. **Adaptive immunity:** B cells (antibodies) and T cells (cytotoxic, helper).
- **Antibody classes:** IgG (most common, crosses the placenta), IgM (first responder), IgA (secretions, breast milk), IgE (allergy).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Type I = immediate (anaphylaxis, IgE).
- The **lymphatic system** returns fluid to the blood and houses immune cells (lymph nodes, spleen, thymus).
- **Reproductive basics:** the menstrual cycle is regulated by FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone; ovulation occurs about midcycle (day 14 of a 28-day cycle). Spermatogenesis occurs in the testes.
> Must know: IgG crosses the placenta and gives a newborn passive immunity.

@lesson PM1 | Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacodynamics | Pharmacology | 6
- **ADME:** absorption, distribution, metabolism (liver, CYP450), excretion (kidney).
- **Half-life:** time for the drug level to fall by half; steady state is reached after about **4 to 5 half-lives**.
- **Therapeutic index:** the margin between effective and toxic doses; narrow-index drugs (digoxin, lithium, warfarin, phenytoin) need level monitoring.
- **First-pass effect:** oral drugs are partly metabolized by the liver before reaching the circulation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** agonists activate receptors; antagonists block them.
- **Loading dose** quickly reaches a therapeutic level; **maintenance doses** keep it.
- Older adults and clients with liver or kidney disease are at higher risk for toxicity.
> Must know: Narrow therapeutic index drugs: think "DLW-P": digoxin, lithium, warfarin, phenytoin.

@lesson PM2 | Cardiovascular Drugs | Pharmacology | 8
- **Beta blockers (-olol):** lower heart rate and BP. Hold for HR under 60 or low BP; do not stop abruptly.
- **ACE inhibitors (-pril):** cough, hyperkalemia, angioedema; avoid in pregnancy.
- **Calcium channel blockers:** amlodipine (peripheral edema), diltiazem and verapamil (lower heart rate; avoid grapefruit juice).
- **Nitrates:** nitroglycerin dilates vessels; sublingual dose may be repeated every 5 minutes for up to 3 doses; call 911 if pain persists after the first dose.
- **Diuretics:** furosemide (loop: K⁺ loss), hydrochlorothiazide (thiazide: K⁺ loss), spironolactone (potassium-sparing: hyperkalemia).
- **Digoxin:** therapeutic level about 0.5 to 2 ng/mL; hypokalemia raises toxicity risk.
- **Statins:** lower LDL; report muscle pain.
> Must know: Potassium-wasting diuretics can trigger digoxin toxicity through hypokalemia.

@lesson PM3 | Anticoagulants and Antiplatelets | Pharmacology | 6
- **Heparin:** IV or subcutaneous, rapid onset; monitor **aPTT**; antidote **protamine sulfate**; watch for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (falling platelets).
- **Enoxaparin (low molecular weight heparin):** subcutaneous; do not expel the air bubble in the prefilled syringe; do not rub the site.
- **Warfarin:** oral; takes days to work; monitor **INR (goal 2 to 3 for most indications)**; antidote **vitamin K**; keep vitamin K intake consistent; many drug interactions.
- **Antiplatelets:** aspirin and clopidogrel prevent platelet aggregation.
- **Thrombolytics (alteplase):** dissolve clots; bleeding is the major risk.
- **Bleeding precautions:** soft toothbrush, electric razor, avoid IM injections, report black stools or hematuria.
> Must know: Heparin → aPTT → protamine. Warfarin → INR → vitamin K.

@lesson PM4 | Endocrine Medications | Pharmacology | 7
- **Insulin types:** rapid-acting (lispro, aspart; onset 10 to 15 min, give with meals), short-acting (regular; the only insulin given IV), intermediate (NPH), long-acting (glargine; do not mix with others).
- Mixing insulins: draw up **clear before cloudy** (regular before NPH).
- **Metformin:** hold around contrast dye; watch for lactic acidosis; GI upset.
- **Levothyroxine:** take on an empty stomach in the morning; do not stop; reports of palpitations or weight loss may indicate too high a dose.
- **Corticosteroids (prednisone):** raise glucose, suppress infection signs, cause fluid retention and bone loss; **never stop abruptly** (adrenal insufficiency); take with food.
- **Hypoglycemia** is the major risk of insulin and sulfonylureas.
> Must know: Clear before cloudy: regular insulin is drawn up before NPH.

@lesson PM5 | Antibiotics | Pharmacology | 6
- Obtain **cultures before the first dose**, when possible.
- **Penicillins and cephalosporins:** ask about allergy; some cross-reactivity exists.
- **Aminoglycosides (gentamicin):** nephrotoxic and ototoxic; monitor peak/trough and kidney function.
- **Vancomycin:** infuse slowly (red man syndrome: flushing from rapid infusion); monitor trough and kidney function.
- **Fluoroquinolones (-floxacin):** tendon rupture risk; avoid taking with antacids or calcium.
- **Tetracyclines:** avoid in pregnancy and children (tooth discoloration); photosensitivity.
- **Metronidazole:** avoid alcohol (disulfiram-like reaction).
- Teach the client to **finish the full course** unless told otherwise. **C. difficile** can follow antibiotic use.
> Must know: Gentamicin and vancomycin both require monitoring of kidney function and drug levels.

@lesson PM6 | Pain Medications | Pharmacology | 6
- **Opioids (morphine, hydromorphone, fentanyl):** respiratory depression, sedation, constipation, nausea; naloxone reverses. Check respiratory rate and sedation before each dose.
- **NSAIDs (ibuprofen, ketorolac):** GI bleeding, kidney injury, increased bleeding risk; take with food; avoid in kidney disease and with anticoagulants.
- **Acetaminophen:** safe for most; hepatotoxic in overdose (usual adult maximum about 4 g/day from all sources, lower in liver disease or heavy alcohol use); antidote acetylcysteine.
- **Aspirin:** avoid in children with viral illness (Reye syndrome).
- **Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA):** only the client presses the button.
- Start a **bowel regimen** with opioids.
> Must know: Opioid-induced constipation is expected, so a stool softener and stimulant are started proactively.

@lesson PM7 | CNS and Psychiatric Medications | Pharmacology | 7
- **Benzodiazepines (lorazepam, diazepam):** sedation, respiratory depression, dependence; do not combine with alcohol/opioids; antidote flumazenil.
- **SSRIs (sertraline, fluoxetine):** 2 to 6 weeks for effect; serotonin syndrome; sexual dysfunction; do not stop abruptly.
- **Antiepileptics:** phenytoin (narrow index; gingival hyperplasia; level 10 to 20 mcg/mL), carbamazepine (monitor blood counts; many interactions), valproate (liver injury, tremor).
- **Lithium:** level 0.6 to 1.2 mEq/L; steady sodium and fluids; toxicity causes coarse tremor and GI symptoms.
- **Antipsychotics:** extrapyramidal symptoms, tardive dyskinesia, NMS; second-generation drugs increase glucose and weight.
- **Stimulants:** monitor growth, blood pressure, and sleep.
> Must know: Never stop antiepileptics, SSRIs, or beta blockers abruptly.

@lesson PM8 | GI, Respiratory Medications, and Safe Practice | Pharmacology | 6
- **Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole):** reduce acid; long-term use risks include C. difficile and low magnesium; give before meals.
- **Antiemetics (ondansetron):** QT prolongation possible.
- **Bronchodilators:** albuterol (short-acting beta agonist; tachycardia, tremor), ipratropium (anticholinergic). In asthma, use the **rescue inhaler first, then the corticosteroid inhaler**; rinse the mouth after inhaled steroids.
- **Metered-dose inhaler:** shake, exhale, inhale slowly while pressing, hold breath about 10 seconds.
- **Medication safety:** check allergies, labs, vital signs; use two identifiers; document right after giving.
- Report **adverse effects** and monitor for **drug interactions** (grapefruit juice, St. John's wort, alcohol).
> Must know: Use the quick-relief (rescue) inhaler before the controller inhaler.

@lesson MS1 | Cardiovascular Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 8
- **Hypertension:** the ACC/AHA guidelines define hypertension as a BP of 130/80 or higher (many other guidelines use 140/90). Teach DASH diet, sodium reduction, weight loss, exercise, and medication adherence.
- **Coronary artery disease and angina:** stable angina is relieved by rest or nitroglycerin; unstable angina and MI are not.
- **MI:** crushing chest pain, diaphoresis, nausea; ST elevation indicates STEMI; troponin rises. Time is muscle: aspirin, ECG, reperfusion.
- **Heart failure:** left-sided (pulmonary congestion) vs. right-sided (peripheral edema, JVD). Treat with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, beta blockers, sodium restriction, daily weights.
- **Peripheral arterial disease:** pain with walking (claudication), cool pale limbs, diminished pulses; **venous disease:** edema, brown skin, warm legs.
> Must know: Daily weights at the same time each morning, after voiding, with similar clothing.

@lesson MS2 | Respiratory Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **COPD** (emphysema, chronic bronchitis): chronic airflow limitation; pursed-lip breathing, low-flow oxygen, smoking cessation, vaccinations.
- **Asthma:** reversible airway inflammation and bronchospasm; wheezing, cough, tight chest; avoid triggers; use a peak flow meter.
- **Pneumonia:** fever, productive cough, crackles; early antibiotics, hydration, deep breathing, oxygen. Aspiration risk in dysphagia.
- **Tuberculosis:** airborne precautions; multi-drug therapy for 6 months or longer; adherence is critical.
- **Pneumothorax and chest tubes:** keep the drainage system **below chest level**; intermittent bubbling in the water-seal chamber (with exhalation or coughing) can be expected early with a pneumothorax, but **continuous** bubbling suggests a persistent air leak that needs evaluation; if the tube disconnects from the drainage system, put the end in sterile water and call for help; if the tube is pulled out of the chest, cover the site with a sterile occlusive dressing as your facility policy directs (often taped on three sides) and call for help right away.
> Must know: Keep the chest drainage system upright and below the level of the chest.

@lesson MS3 | Diabetes and Thyroid Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **Type 1:** autoimmune, no insulin; **Type 2:** insulin resistance, often managed with lifestyle and oral drugs.
- **Hypoglycemia:** shaky, sweaty, hungry, confused; rule of 15. **Hyperglycemia:** polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia.
- **A1C** reflects 2 to 3 months of control; goal for many adults is below 7%.
- **Sick-day rules:** continue insulin, check glucose more often, drink fluids.
- **Foot care:** inspect daily, never walk barefoot, moisturize but not between toes.
- **Hypothyroidism:** fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, bradycardia, constipation. **Hyperthyroidism (Graves):** weight loss, heat intolerance, tachycardia, exophthalmos, tremor.
- **Thyroid storm:** fever, severe tachycardia, agitation; a medical emergency.
> Must know: Hypothyroid = slow and cold. Hyperthyroid = fast and hot.

@lesson MS4 | GI and Hepatic Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **GERD:** avoid late meals, elevate head of bed, avoid triggers (fatty foods, caffeine, alcohol).
- **Peptic ulcer disease:** *H. pylori* treated with antibiotics plus a PPI; avoid NSAIDs; black tarry stool (melena) suggests upper GI bleeding.
- **Pancreatitis:** severe epigastric pain radiating to the back; elevated lipase and amylase; NPO, pain control, fluids.
- **Cirrhosis:** ascites, varices, jaundice, hepatic encephalopathy (elevated ammonia → confusion, asterixis); lactulose lowers ammonia; protein is not eliminated entirely.
- **Appendicitis:** right lower quadrant pain; do not apply heat; do not give laxatives; NPO.
- **Bowel obstruction:** colicky pain, vomiting, distention, absent bowel sounds late; NG decompression.
> Must know: Lactulose reduces ammonia in hepatic encephalopathy; the goal is 2 to 3 soft stools per day.

@lesson MS5 | Renal and Urinary Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **UTI:** dysuria, frequency, urgency; fluids, antibiotics, front-to-back hygiene. Older adults may present with confusion.
- **Acute kidney injury:** rising creatinine and BUN, oliguria; phases: oliguric, diuretic, recovery. Avoid nephrotoxic drugs; monitor potassium.
- **Chronic kidney disease:** anemia, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypertension, fluid overload.
- **Hemodialysis:** protect the **fistula arm**: no BP or venipuncture; check for a thrill and bruit.
- **Peritoneal dialysis:** infection (peritonitis) is the key complication: cloudy outflow, fever, abdominal pain.
- **Kidney stones:** severe flank pain; strain urine; increase fluids.
> Must know: Never take a blood pressure or draw blood from the arm with a dialysis fistula.

@lesson MS6 | Neurological Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **Ischemic stroke:** clot; **hemorrhagic stroke:** bleed. Assess with FAST; time of onset determines thrombolytic eligibility. Keep NPO until a swallow screen is passed.
- **Seizures:** protect, time, side-lying afterward; document the type.
- **Multiple sclerosis:** demyelination; relapsing symptoms; fatigue and heat worsen symptoms.
- **Parkinson disease:** tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, shuffling gait; levodopa-carbidopa; protein can reduce absorption.
- **Myasthenia gravis:** muscle weakness worse with activity, ptosis; give anticholinesterase meds on time.
- **Guillain-Barré:** ascending paralysis; monitor respiratory function.
- **Meningitis:** fever, headache, neck stiffness, photophobia; droplet precautions.
> Must know: In Guillain-Barré, the greatest risk is respiratory failure from ascending paralysis.

@lesson MS7 | Musculoskeletal Disorders | Med-Surg Basics | 6
- **Fractures:** assess the **six Ps**: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, poikilothermia (coolness).
- **Compartment syndrome:** pain out of proportion, unrelieved by medication, tight swelling, paresthesia; an emergency: do not elevate above the heart; notify the provider immediately (fasciotomy).
- **Fat embolism:** after long bone fracture; sudden dyspnea, confusion, petechiae.
- **Osteoporosis:** calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention.
- **Osteoarthritis:** pain with activity, joint stiffness lasting under 30 minutes. **Rheumatoid arthritis:** symmetric joint swelling, morning stiffness over an hour.
- **Gout:** uric acid; avoid organ meats and alcohol; fluids.
- **Hip replacement precautions:** avoid hip flexion beyond 90 degrees, adduction, and internal rotation.
> Must know: Pain out of proportion that is unrelieved by opioids suggests compartment syndrome.

@lesson MS8 | Sepsis and Shock | Med-Surg Basics | 7
- **Sepsis:** the body's dysregulated response to infection; signs include fever or low temperature, tachycardia, tachypnea, confusion, hypotension. **Septic shock:** persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors.
- **Early treatment bundle:** blood cultures, lactate, broad-spectrum antibiotics, IV fluids (30 mL/kg for hypotension), then vasopressors as ordered.
- **Types of shock:** hypovolemic (volume loss), cardiogenic (pump failure), distributive (septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic), obstructive (PE, tamponade).
- **Anaphylaxis:** airway swelling, hypotension; epinephrine IM is first-line.
- **Neurogenic shock:** hypotension with **bradycardia** and warm, dry skin after a spinal cord injury.
- **Monitor** mental status, urine output, lactate, and blood pressure.
> Must know: Anaphylaxis is treated first with IM epinephrine.

@lesson FU1 | The Nursing Process and Documentation | Fundamentals | 6
- ADPIE: Assessment, Diagnosis, Planning, Implementation, Evaluation.
- **Subjective data** is what the client says (pain, nausea); **objective data** is measurable (vital signs, lab results).
- **Nursing diagnoses** use NANDA: problem, etiology ("related to"), and defining characteristics.

@lesson FU2 | Vital Signs and Head-to-Toe Assessment | Fundamentals | 8
Typical adult ranges at rest: **temperature** 97.8 to 99.1°F (36.5 to 37.3°C), **pulse** 60 to 100, **respirations** 12 to 20, **BP** under 120/80, **SpO₂** 95 to 100%.
- Count respirations without the client knowing. Measure pulse for a full minute if irregular.
- **Pain** is the fifth vital sign in many settings.
## Assessment order
Inspection, **palpation**, percussion, auscultation, except in the abdomen, where you **auscultate before** palpating or percussing.
- Orthostatic BP: lying, then sitting/standing; a drop of 20 mmHg systolic or 10 diastolic with symptoms is significant.
- **Level of consciousness** is the most sensitive indicator of neurological change.
> Must know: Auscultate the abdomen BEFORE palpation, since touch can change bowel sounds.

@lesson FU3 | Infection Prevention and Hygiene | Fundamentals | 6
- **Chain of infection:** agent → reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host. Break any link.
- **Hand hygiene:** alcohol-based rub for 20 seconds when hands are not visibly soiled; soap and water for at least 20 seconds when soiled and for C. difficile.
- **Standard precautions** are used for every client.
- **Bathing:** wash from clean to dirty, and from head to toe; assess skin; provide privacy; promote independence.
- **Oral care** reduces aspiration pneumonia; use side-lying for unconscious clients.
- **Healthcare-associated infections:** CAUTI, CLABSI, ventilator-associated pneumonia, surgical site infection; bundles reduce risk.
> Must know: Hand hygiene is the single most effective way to prevent infection spread.

@lesson FU4 | Safety and Body Mechanics | Fundamentals | 6
- **Body mechanics:** keep a wide base of support, bend at the knees, keep the load close, avoid twisting, and use assistive devices.
- **Lifting/transfers:** gait belts, mechanical lifts, and enough staff.
- **Falls:** assess risk on admission, use alarms, ensure a clear path.
- **Positioning:** supine (flat on the back), prone (stomach), lateral, Fowler's (head up 45 to 60 degrees), semi-Fowler's (30 to 45), Sims (semi-prone).
- **Range of motion:** active (client moves), passive (nurse moves the joint); do not force.
- **Assistive devices:** a walker is used with the weak side supported; crutches: support on the hands, not the axillae.
> Must know: Bear weight on the hands, not the armpits, when using crutches.

@lesson FU5 | Communication and Patient Education | Fundamentals | 6
- **Therapeutic communication:** active listening, open-ended questions, silence, clarifying, and empathy.
- **Interpreters:** use a qualified medical interpreter, not family; speak to the client, not the interpreter.
- **Teaching:** assess learning needs and readiness, set goals with the client, use plain language, and teach-back. Use multiple methods (demonstration, handouts).
- **Barriers:** pain, anxiety, language, literacy, hearing or vision impairment, cultural beliefs.
- **Cultural humility:** ask about preferences; do not assume.
- Teach in short sessions, most important information first.
> Must know: Use a professional interpreter rather than a family member for health information.

@lesson FU6 | Nutrition, Hydration, and Elimination | Fundamentals | 7
- **Macronutrients:** carbohydrates (4 kcal/g), protein (4 kcal/g), fat (9 kcal/g).
- **BMI** = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²; normal 18.5 to 24.9.
- **Fluid needs** about 1.5 to 2 L/day for many adults; watch for dehydration (dry mucosa, poor skin turgor, concentrated urine, tachycardia).
- **Daily weight** is the best indicator of fluid balance.
- **Therapeutic diets:** low sodium (heart failure), carbohydrate-controlled (diabetes), renal (limit K, P, Na), low-residue, clear liquids.
- **Constipation:** increase fluids, fiber, and activity.
- **Urinary elimination:** normal output about 30 mL/hr or more; assess for retention (bladder scan).
> Must know: Weigh the client daily to track fluid status: 1 kg is about 1 L of fluid.

@lesson FU7 | Wound Care and Skin Integrity | Fundamentals | 6
- **Wound healing phases:** hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, maturation (remodeling).
- **Intention:** primary (edges approximated, sutures), secondary (heals from the base up), tertiary (delayed closure).
- **Dressing choices:** moist wound healing is best; hydrocolloids for light drainage, foam or alginate for heavy drainage; dry sterile gauze for dry surgical wounds.
- **Drains:** record output; keep the bulb compressed.
- **Signs of wound infection:** redness, warmth, swelling, purulent drainage, odor, fever.
- **Clean technique** for chronic wounds; **sterile technique** for surgical wounds, per policy.
- Nutrition (protein, vitamin C, zinc) supports healing.
> Must know: Keep wound beds moist and surrounding skin dry; never use dry gauze that adheres to a granulating wound.

@lesson FU8 | Ethics, Legal Issues, and Professionalism | Fundamentals | 6
- **Scope of practice** is defined by the Nurse Practice Act and facility policy.
- **Negligence** requires duty, breach, causation, and harm; **malpractice** is professional negligence.
- **Informed consent** requires disclosure, understanding, and voluntariness.
- **Advance directives** include living will and durable power of attorney for health care. **DNR** orders require a provider order.
- **Patient rights:** privacy, respect, refusal of care, access to information.
- **Professional boundaries:** no social media sharing of client information; no personal relationships with clients; no accepting valuable gifts.
- **Impaired colleague:** report according to policy to protect clients.
> Must know: A DNR order must be written by a provider; the nurse cannot assume one.
