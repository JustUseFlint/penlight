@tag S1
Q: Which vessels carry oxygenated blood from the lungs back to the heart?
- Pulmonary arteries
- Vena cava
+ Pulmonary veins
- Coronary arteries
R: Pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. Pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the vena cava returns deoxygenated blood from the body.
Q: Which valve lies between the left atrium and the left ventricle?
- Tricuspid valve
+ Mitral (bicuspid) valve
- Aortic valve
- Pulmonic valve
R: The mitral valve separates the left atrium from the left ventricle. The tricuspid valve is on the right side, and the aortic and pulmonic valves guard the exits of the ventricles.
Q: Where does gas exchange in the lungs occur?
- Bronchi
- Trachea
- Bronchioles
+ Alveoli
R: Oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across the thin walls of the alveoli and the surrounding capillaries.
Q: Which blood component is primarily responsible for clotting?
- Red blood cells
- White blood cells
+ Platelets
- Plasma proteins only
R: Platelets (thrombocytes) form plugs and start the clotting cascade. Red cells carry oxygen, and white cells defend against infection.
Q: What happens to the diaphragm and chest volume during inhalation?
- The diaphragm relaxes and chest volume decreases.
+ The diaphragm contracts and chest volume increases.
- The diaphragm contracts and chest volume decreases.
- The diaphragm relaxes and chest volume increases.
R: During inhalation the diaphragm contracts and flattens, which enlarges the chest cavity and lowers the pressure so that air flows in.
@tag S2
Q: Which part of the brain is chiefly responsible for balance and coordination?
- Cerebrum
+ Cerebellum
- Brainstem
- Hypothalamus
R: The cerebellum coordinates movement and balance. The brainstem controls breathing and heart rate, and the hypothalamus regulates temperature and hunger.
Q: Which hormone, released by the pancreas, lowers blood glucose?
- Glucagon
- Cortisol
+ Insulin
- Epinephrine
R: Insulin moves glucose into cells and lowers blood glucose. Glucagon raises it.
Q: Which response is part of the sympathetic nervous system's "fight or flight" reaction?
- Decreased heart rate
+ Increased heart rate
- Increased digestion
- Constricted pupils
R: Sympathetic activation raises heart rate and dilates pupils, and slows digestion. Parasympathetic activation does the opposite ("rest and digest").
Q: The part of a neuron that receives signals from other neurons is the
+ dendrite.
- axon.
- myelin sheath.
- synaptic vesicle.
R: Dendrites receive input, and the axon sends the signal onward. Myelin insulates the axon and speeds conduction.
Q: Which gland is often called the "master gland" because it controls other endocrine glands?
- Thyroid
- Adrenal
- Pancreas
+ Pituitary
R: The pituitary releases hormones (such as TSH) that regulate other glands, under direction from the hypothalamus.
@tag S3
Q: Which organ produces bile?
- Gallbladder
- Pancreas
+ Liver
- Stomach
R: The liver makes bile and the gallbladder stores and concentrates it. The pancreas makes digestive enzymes.
Q: Where does most nutrient absorption take place?
- Stomach
+ Small intestine
- Large intestine
- Esophagus
R: The villi of the small intestine absorb most nutrients. The large intestine absorbs mostly water.
Q: Which enzyme begins the chemical digestion of starch in the mouth?
+ Amylase
- Pepsin
- Lipase
- Trypsin
R: Salivary amylase starts breaking down starch. Pepsin digests protein in the stomach, and lipase digests fat.
Q: Which is the correct path of urine flow out of the body?
- Kidney, bladder, ureter, urethra
+ Kidney, ureter, bladder, urethra
- Ureter, kidney, bladder, urethra
- Kidney, urethra, bladder, ureter
R: The kidneys make urine, the ureters carry it to the bladder, and the urethra carries it out of the body.
Q: Where does fertilization normally occur?
- Uterus
- Ovary
+ Fallopian tube
- Cervix
R: Fertilization typically occurs in the fallopian tube, and the embryo implants in the uterus.

Q: Which structure connects muscle to bone?
+ Tendon
- Ligament
- Cartilage
- Fascia sheath only
R: Tendons connect muscle to bone, and ligaments connect bone to bone.
Q: Which type of muscle is involuntary and found in the walls of the stomach?
- Skeletal
- Cardiac
+ Smooth
- Striated
R: Smooth muscle is involuntary and lines organs and vessels. Cardiac muscle is involuntary but only in the heart, and skeletal muscle is voluntary.
Q: Which layer of the skin contains blood vessels, nerves, and glands?
- Epidermis
+ Dermis
- Hypodermis
- Stratum corneum
R: The dermis contains blood vessels, nerve endings, hair follicles, and glands. The epidermis is the outer barrier, and the hypodermis is mostly fat.
Q: A child receives a vaccine and later makes antibodies against the disease. This is an example of
- passive natural immunity.
+ active immunity.
- passive artificial immunity.
- innate immunity.
R: Vaccines stimulate the body to make its own antibodies, which is active immunity. Passive immunity comes from receiving antibodies.
Q: Which cells produce antibodies?
- T cells
- Platelets
+ B cells
- Macrophages
R: B cells (plasma cells) make antibodies. T cells attack infected cells directly, and macrophages engulf pathogens.
@tag S5
Q: Which organelle produces most of the cell's ATP?
- Ribosome
+ Mitochondrion
- Golgi apparatus
- Lysosome
R: Mitochondria carry out cellular respiration to make ATP. Ribosomes build proteins.
Q: Which process moves molecules from an area of low concentration to an area of high concentration?
- Diffusion
- Osmosis
- Facilitated diffusion
+ Active transport
R: Moving against the concentration gradient requires energy (ATP), which is active transport. Diffusion and osmosis move substances down the gradient with no energy.
Q: Which structure is found in plant cells but not in animal cells?
- Mitochondria
- Nucleus
+ Cell wall
- Ribosomes
R: Plant cells have a cell wall and chloroplasts. Animal cells and plant cells both have a nucleus, mitochondria, and ribosomes.
Q: Which type of molecule is the main source of quick energy?
+ Carbohydrates
- Nucleic acids
- Proteins
- Vitamins
R: Carbohydrates are the body's main quick fuel. Proteins mainly build structures and enzymes, and nucleic acids store genetic information.
Q: Which organelle packages and ships proteins out of the cell?
- Lysosome
+ Golgi apparatus
- Nucleolus
- Mitochondrion
R: The Golgi apparatus modifies, packages, and ships proteins. Lysosomes digest waste.

Q: In a cross between two heterozygous (Bb × Bb) parents, what percent of offspring are expected to show the recessive phenotype?
- 0%
+ 25%
- 50%
- 75%
R: Bb × Bb gives 25% BB, 50% Bb, and 25% bb. Only bb shows the recessive trait.
Q: Which base pairs with adenine in DNA?
- Cytosine
- Guanine
+ Thymine
- Uracil
R: In DNA, A pairs with T and C pairs with G. Uracil replaces thymine in RNA.
Q: An organism with the genotype Bb is
- homozygous dominant.
- homozygous recessive.
+ heterozygous.
- a phenotype.
R: Two different alleles make a heterozygous genotype.
Q: Which treatment is effective against bacterial infections but not viral infections?
- Vaccines
+ Antibiotics
- Antiviral drugs
- Rest
R: Antibiotics kill or stop bacteria. Viruses are not affected by antibiotics.
Q: Meiosis results in
- two identical diploid cells.
- two cells with half the chromosomes.
+ four cells with half the chromosomes.
- four identical diploid cells.
R: Meiosis produces four genetically different sex cells with half the number of chromosomes.

Q: An atom has 6 protons and 8 neutrons. What is its mass number?
- 6
- 8

- 2
R: Mass number = protons + neutrons = 6 + 8 = 14.
Q: Which type of bond involves the transfer of electrons from one atom to another?
- Covalent bond
+ Ionic bond
- Hydrogen bond
- Peptide bond
R: In an ionic bond (such as NaCl), one atom gives electrons to another. Covalent bonds share electrons.
Q: Which of the following is a chemical change?
- Ice melting
- Water evaporating
+ Iron rusting
- Sugar dissolving in water
R: Rusting creates a new substance (iron oxide). Melting, evaporating, and dissolving change form but not the substance's identity.
Q: Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are called
- ions.
+ isotopes.
- molecules.
- compounds.
R: Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
Q: An atom that loses one electron becomes
- a negative ion.
- a neutral atom.
+ a positive ion.
- an isotope.
R: Losing a negatively charged electron leaves more protons than electrons, so the ion is positive.

Q: A solution has a pH of 3. Compared with a solution with a pH of 5, it is
- 2 times more acidic.
- 20 times more acidic.
+ 100 times more acidic.
- 100 times more basic.
R: Each pH unit is a tenfold change. Two units lower is 10 × 10 = 100 times more acidic.
Q: Which pH value is basic?
- 6.5
- 7.0
+ 8.2
- 2.0
R: A pH above 7 is basic (alkaline). A pH of 7 is neutral, and values below 7 are acidic.
Q: In a saltwater solution, the salt is the
+ solute.
- solvent.
- solution.
- catalyst.
R: The solute is the substance that dissolves, and the solvent (water) does the dissolving.
Q: The normal pH range of human arterial blood is
- 6.35 to 6.45
+ 7.35 to 7.45
- 7.0 to 7.5
- 7.8 to 8.0
R: Blood is slightly basic, tightly maintained between 7.35 and 7.45.
Q: A neutralization reaction between an acid and a base produces
- two acids.
+ water and a salt.
- hydrogen gas only.
- two bases.
R: Acid + base → water + salt.

Q: A student tests whether plant growth depends on the amount of water by giving three groups of plants 50, 100, and 150 mL daily. What is the dependent variable?
- The amount of water
- The type of plant
+ The plant growth
- The amount of sunlight
R: The dependent variable is what is measured, which is plant growth. The amount of water is the independent variable, and the sunlight and plant type should be controlled.
Q: What is the purpose of a control group?
- To increase the sample size
+ To provide a baseline for comparison
- To change several variables at once
- To prove the hypothesis is true
R: A control group does not receive the treatment, so the effect of the treatment can be compared against it.
Q: Which statement is a testable hypothesis?
- Music is beautiful.
+ If plants are played music for an hour a day, they will grow taller than plants without music.
- Plants are better than animals.
- Why do plants grow?
R: A hypothesis is a testable, falsifiable prediction. The other options are opinions or questions.
Q: Two events occur together in a data set. What can a scientist conclude from correlation alone?
- One event caused the other.
+ The events are associated, but causation is not proven.
- The events are unrelated.
- The data are invalid.
R: Correlation shows an association, but a controlled experiment is needed to show causation.
Q: Which would most increase the reliability of an experiment's results?
- Using one trial
+ Repeating the experiment several times
- Changing the procedure each time
- Using a smaller sample
R: Repeated trials and larger samples reduce the effect of chance.

Q: Choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement.
- The box of supplies are empty.
+ The box of supplies is empty.
- The boxes of supplies is empty.
- The box of supplies were empty.
R: The subject is "box" (singular), so the verb is "is." The phrase "of supplies" is a prepositional phrase and does not change the subject.
Q: Which verb correctly completes the sentence? "Neither the nurses nor the doctor ____ late."
- are
+ is
- were
- have been
R: With "neither/nor," the verb agrees with the closer subject, "doctor" (singular), so "is" is correct.
Q: In the sentence "The patient quickly drank the juice," the word "quickly" is a(n)
- adjective.
- noun.
+ adverb.
- preposition.
R: "Quickly" describes the verb "drank," so it is an adverb.
Q: Which sentence uses the correct verb?
- Everyone have finished the exam.
- Each of the students are ready.
+ Everyone has finished the exam.
- Somebody were here.
R: "Everyone," "each," and "somebody" are singular and need singular verbs ("has," "is," "was").
Q: Which word in this sentence is a preposition? "The chart is on the desk."
- chart
- is
+ on
- desk
R: A preposition shows a relationship between a noun and another word (here, location).
Q: Which sentence has a compound subject that requires a plural verb?
- The nurse is on break.
+ The doctor and the nurse are reviewing the chart.
- Each nurse has a badge.
- The team is meeting.
R: Subjects joined by "and" are plural, so they take "are."

Q: Which sentence is a comma splice?
- The patient slept, and the nurse charted.
+ The patient slept, the nurse charted.
- The patient slept; the nurse charted.
- The patient slept while the nurse charted.
R: A comma splice joins two independent clauses with only a comma. Fix it with a conjunction, a semicolon, or a period.
Q: Which sentence uses a semicolon correctly?
- The nurse checked the chart; and then left.
+ The nurse checked the chart; the doctor reviewed the labs.
- The nurse checked; the chart.
- Although tired; the nurse stayed.
R: A semicolon joins two complete, related sentences.
Q: Which sentence is a fragment?
- The medication works quickly.
- Take the pill with water.
+ Because the patient was nauseated.
- She left.
R: A dependent clause beginning with "because" cannot stand alone, since it is not a complete thought.
Q: Which is the correct use of an apostrophe?
- The nurses' chart's were lost.
+ The nurse's chart was lost.
- The nurse chart's was lost.
- The nurses chart was lost.
R: "Nurse's chart" shows that the chart belongs to one nurse. Apostrophes are not used to make plurals.
Q: Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
- When the alarm sounded the nurse responded.
+ When the alarm sounded, the nurse responded.
- When the alarm sounded; the nurse responded.
- When, the alarm sounded the nurse responded.
R: Use a comma after an introductory dependent clause.
Q: A sentence with one independent clause and one dependent clause is called
- simple.
- compound.
+ complex.
- a fragment.
R: A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.

Q: "Please do not ____ your badge." Which word fits?

R: "Lose" is a verb meaning to misplace. "Loose" means not tight.
Q: Which sentences are written correctly? Select all that apply.
